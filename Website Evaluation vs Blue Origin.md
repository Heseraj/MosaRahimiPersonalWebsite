## Critical Gaps & Recommended Fixes

### 1. No Explicit "Chief of Staff" or "Executive Advisory" Framing

**Problem:** The site uses "operations leader" and "coordination supervisor" but never surfaces "Chief of Staff" as a named competency — despite the Afghan Border Police CoS title being buried in the timeline. Tony's team wants someone who has *advised senior leaders on strategic decisions*. The site doesn't foreground this.

**Fix:** Add a capability chip labeled **"Executive Advisory"** or **"Chief of Staff Operations"** alongside "Operations Leadership" in the skills/tags area. Consider a one-liner in the hero or About section: *"Former Chief of Staff to a general officer; now building the same advisory muscle in aerospace manufacturing."*

---

### 2. Current Title Undersells the Trajectory

**Problem:** "Coordination Supervisor" reads as junior at Blue Origin's L7/Director-track level. The recruiter already knows the story, but a hiring manager reviewing the site cold will see "Supervisor" prominently and may question the seniority match.

**Fix:** Lead the Kuiper Work section with *scope and impact* rather than the Amazon title:
- Cross-value-stream coordination (OTAC + Decks & Panels)
- Production system design and operational tracking architecture
- NPI build support for satellite constellation manufacturing
- Shift transformation: inherited lowest-performing team → top performer in 5 months

---

### 3. Missing: Strategic / Executive Communication Artifacts

**Problem:** Blue Origin wants someone who can own MBRs, synthesize for VP/SVP-level audiences, and drive executive decisions. The "Technical Work" section shows GitHub repos (neural networks, R-based risk modeling) — signaling *technical curiosity* but not *executive advisory capability*.

**Fix:** Add a brief artifact or case study demonstrating executive communication — options:
- A sanitized operational review framework
- A strategic planning one-pager
- A "How I Communicate" section that bridges floor-level data → executive synthesis
- A sample decision memo structure (even abstracted)

---

### 4. AI/ML Positioning Creates Signal Confusion

**Problem:** The page title is "Learning How Machines Learn" and the hero tags are "Artificial Intelligence, Deep Learning, Economic Modeling, Data Science." For a Chief of Staff / Operations Advisory role, this framing raises the question: *Is this person pursuing AI/ML roles, or operations leadership?*

**Fix:** Reposition AI as a *complement* to operations, not the primary identity:
- Change hero tags to: **Operations Strategy · Aerospace Manufacturing · Applied AI · Systems Leadership**
- Add a clarifying line: *"AI and data science are how I sharpen operational systems — not a departure from them."*
- Consider whether the page title itself ("Learning How Machines Learn") serves this audience or confuses it.

---

### 5. No Supply Chain, Quality, or Mission Assurance Language

**Problem:** The Blue Origin role explicitly covers Integrated Supply Chain, Test Ops, Safety, Quality, and Mission Assurance. The Kuiper work touches these areas, but the website doesn't use this vocabulary anywhere.

**Fix:** In the Kuiper role descriptions, incorporate:
- "Production readiness and quality systems"
- "Supply chain coordination across manufacturing value streams"
- "Mission assurance-adjacent accountability in cleanroom aerospace environments"
- "Cross-functional alignment with quality, engineering, and supply chain teams"

---

### 6. "PIVOT" Framing Creates Departure Risk Signal

**Problem:** A dedicated "PIVOT" section positions you as someone *leaving* operations for AI. For a recruiter evaluating a long-term Director-track operations hire, this reads as: "This candidate is on their way out of operations."

**Fix:**
- Rename the nav label from **"PIVOT"** to **"AI + OPS"** or **"EXPANSION"**
- The body copy already says "Not reinvention for its own sake. A deliberate expansion of how I solve." — let the section title match that framing.
- Remove any language that implies a *departure* from operations leadership.

---

### 7. Digital Twin Section — Irrelevant for This Audience

**Problem:** A senior hiring manager reviewing candidates doesn't need to chat with an AI twin. It's a creative portfolio piece, but for *this evaluator*, it dilutes the page's seriousness and raises the "is this person an AI hobbyist?" question.

**Fix:** Either:
- Move it to a secondary page / footer Easter egg
- Add a contextual framing line: *"An experiment in applied AI — the same curiosity I bring to manufacturing systems."*
- Or simply ensure it doesn't appear in the primary nav for the version of the page Jasmine sees

## Summary

The site is beautifully crafted and tells a compelling personal story. For a *general audience*, it succeeds. But for the specific Blue Origin evaluators (recruiters and hiring managers reviewing you for a Director-track Chief of Staff to the SVP of Operations), it introduces unnecessary ambiguity around three questions:

1. **Identity:** Is this person pursuing AI/ML or operations leadership?
2. **Altitude:** Does "Supervisor" reflect their actual operating level?
3. **Toolkit:** Do they have the executive advisory capabilities we need?

The strongest version of this site for this evaluation pushes **CoS archetype + strategic operations advisory + aerospace manufacturing leadership** forward, with AI as a clearly complementary capability rather than the headline identity.
